Zapier vs Make:
make the workflow work.

Compare Zapier and Make by the apps, steps and operating costs your automation needs. Use a practical test before choosing a plan.

By WHATIF · Reviewed October 16, 2026

The quick answer

Start by checking your exact app connections in both products. Consider Zapier if its Zap workflow approach fits the job and the team maintaining it. Consider Make if mapping a scenario into modules and routes suits how you want to build it. Both support branching logic; the useful choice is the workflow you can operate reliably at an acceptable cost.

Method: based on the official documentation linked below, with WHATIF's editorial advice on choosing a workflow. We have not run a head-to-head benchmark or measured time savings. Features, plan limits and prices can change.

Start with one repetitive job

Write the job in plain language: “When a new inquiry arrives, add its details to our working list and notify the right person.” Then name the source app, destination app, required fields and the condition for sending the notification.

Check the specific actions you need, not just whether the platform lists an app. Creating a record, updating one and finding an existing one are different requirements. Note who owns the connection and who will fix a failed run.

Tasks and credits are different units

One finished business process can contain several billable actions or module operations. Comparing a plan's task allowance with another plan's credit allowance does not, by itself, establish which costs less.

Zapier documents successful action steps as tasks, while triggers and tools such as Filter and Paths do not use tasks. Some AI, code and other products have different rates.2 Make documents fixed and dynamic credit usage, including rates affected by AI tokens and other consumption factors.5

A cost estimate you can check

Illustration only: a Zap using two standard billable actions for each successful submission would use 200 tasks for 100 submissions, before accounting for replays or differently priced features. For Make, inspect the modules and their actual credit rates rather than assuming that the same job consumes two credits.

Run a small sample, read the usage log, and estimate your expected monthly volume from that result. Add likely repeats, error recovery, connected-app costs and any separate AI-provider charges. Compare those totals against current plan terms.

A useful first test

This is a suggested exercise, not a workflow WHATIF has tested for these vendors. Use sample records and a test destination:

  1. Create a sample inquiry with a name, a request and a unique reference.
  2. Copy it into your chosen list or CRM using the exact action you need.
  3. Route a notification based on one simple condition.
  4. Try a record with a missing field, then replay an earlier record to check for duplicates.
  5. Read the run history and usage totals. Write down how you would identify and fix a failure.

Have the person who will maintain it explain the flow back to you. A workflow that only its original builder understands can become an operating cost of its own.

When is it worth paying?

Pay for a known requirement: a needed integration, appropriate schedule, higher volume or team controls. Check the plan's billing period and what happens when usage exceeds the included allowance. Use the provider's current checkout rather than relying on an old comparison's headline price.

Which is cheaper, Zapier or Make?

It depends on the workflow, volume and enabled features. Measure the same job in each system, then compare the relevant plan and any extra usage. A larger allowance expressed in a different unit does not prove a lower cost.

Can both branch a workflow?

Yes. Zapier documents Filter and Paths tools; Make documents routers and filters. Check the current plan and the exact behavior required for your process.34
